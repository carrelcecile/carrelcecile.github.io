About

I research the geopolitics of AI. My current work centres on AI compute and on how open-source, open-weight and closed models should be governed: Europe's dependencies in the compute stack, how export controls and US–China rivalry constrain its options, and how human rights law applies to the rules for powerful models. I recently completed a Master of Public Policy at the London School of Economics, with Distinction, and I now focus my work on research and advice in this field.

Previously, I have worked with multiple international institutions, including the International Crisis Group, the World Economic Forum and UNICEF. I have supported their work across a range of projects, including research, analytical writing, communications, data and statistics, all contributing to policy.

I have presented policy recommendations on digital identity to experts at Chatham House, the Alan Turing Institute and the Tony Blair Institute, and I bring a technical background in computer science and machine learning to policy questions.

I am French and Thai, grew up in New York, Switzerland and Hong Kong, and live in London. I work in French and English.

Speaker bio

Cécile Carrel is a researcher in the geopolitics of AI. She holds a Master of Public Policy from the London School of Economics, with Distinction. Her current work centres on AI compute, the governance of open-source, open-weight and closed models, and Europe's strategic dependencies. At the International Crisis Group she contributed to published analysis for the UN Security Council, the EU External Action Service and foreign ministries; she has built analytics reported to the World Economic Forum's Managing Board, and presented policy recommendations to experts at Chatham House, the Alan Turing Institute and the Tony Blair Institute. She brings a technical background in computer science and machine learning to policy analysis. French and Thai, she is based in London.

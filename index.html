Cécile Carrel, researcher in the geopolitics of AI

Cécile Carrel is a researcher working on the geopolitics of AI, with a focus on compute, the governance of open-weight models and Europe’s strategic dependencies. She began her career in engineering and data, with a background in computer science and machine learning, before moving into policy through the World Economic Forum, the International Crisis Group and UNICEF.

She holds a Master of Public Policy from the London School of Economics, with Distinction, and is now a researcher at Augur AI, where she studies the positions of France and Sweden in the AI supply chain. French and Thai, she grew up in New York, Switzerland and Hong Kong, and is based in London.

Focus

Cécile researches and advises on four themes, for governments, institutions and companies.

AI compute and geopolitical dominance

Who controls chips and computing capacity, and what that means for the United States, China and Europe.

Open-weight models and human rights

How human rights law bears on the rules governing openly released models.

Europe’s strategic dependencies

How export controls and economic security shape Europe’s room for manoeuvre between Washington and Beijing.

Technology in conflict

Autonomous weapons, maritime security and the diplomacy of ASEAN.
